Good to know

Tell me about:

The Adjustable Dog Wheelchair (Between 4-11kg) gives dogs with paralysis or significant rear-leg weakness back their independence, the ability to walk, sniff, explore and be a dog again. It's often recommended for conditions like IVDD, degenerative myelopathy, severe hip dysplasia or spinal injury, where the front legs are still strong but the rear end needs full or partial support. Many dogs adapt within days, especially with a gradual, positive introduction.

Key features

Fully adjustable frame

Height, length and width all adjust independently, so the wheelchair grows and adapts with your dog rather than needing replacing as their needs change.

Supports two use modes

Works as a partial-support aid for dogs with weak hind legs who still walk with help, or as full rear-end support for paralysed dogs who can't bear weight at all.

Lightweight, durable build

An aluminium frame keeps the chair light enough for your dog to manoeuvre easily, while staying sturdy for daily outdoor use.

Designed for toileting freedom

The open frame design lets your dog relieve themselves normally while wearing it, no lifting in and out needed.

Getting the fit right

Sized on the gap between your dog's belly and the ground.

Measure your dog's weight and the distance from belly to ground as shown in our size guide before ordering, getting this measurement right is the most important step. Introduce the wheelchair gradually in a familiar, flat space, using treats and encouragement, before progressing to walks outdoors.

Lightweight aluminium frame with padded, adjustable harness straps and wheels sized to the weight band.

For external use as a supportive aid, not a medical device, always follow the fitting guide and check your pet's skin daily for rubbing or soreness, loosening the fit if needed. Remove before unsupervised play, swimming (unless designed for it) or overnight wear unless your vet has advised otherwise. Machine washable on a gentle cycle, air dry. Keep away from puppies who may chew straps or buckles. If your pet has an open wound, check with your vet before fitting anything over the area.

How do I know if my dog needs full or partial support?

Dogs who can still move their hind legs but struggle to bear full weight usually need partial support; fully paralysed dogs need the chair to take their complete rear-end weight. Your vet or a canine physiotherapist can help confirm which setup is right.

How long does it take my dog to adjust?

Most dogs adapt within a few short sessions, especially with a calm, encouraging introduction on familiar ground before heading outside.

Can it be adjusted as my dog's condition changes?

Yes, height, length and width all adjust independently, so the same wheelchair can be reconfigured as your dog's needs change, rather than needing to buy a new one.

Is it suitable for toileting?

Yes, the open frame design is built specifically so your dog can toilet normally without needing to be lifted out.
