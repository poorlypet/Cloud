Itchy skin

The number one reason owners come to us.

What it is

An over-reaction to something ordinary. Pollen, grass, dust mites or a food. The skin gets inflamed, the dog scratches, the scratching makes it worse.

Rule out the causes ›

What to look for

Scratching that wakes them at night. One paw licked rusty brown. Pink skin on the belly and armpits. Flakes on the bed.

Check the signs ›

What actually helps

Omega 3 every day to calm the skin from inside. A gentle wash and a balm on the outside. A flea routine underneath it all. Most dogs settle in two to three weeks.

See the plan ›

When to see the vet

Sudden swelling or hives, a face that puffs up, round bald patches, or a hot weepy patch that spreads. Today, not tomorrow.

Understanding skin and allergy support

Skin and allergy issues can show up as itching, dryness, licking, redness, flaky skin, rough paw pads or a dry nose. Supportive products can help soothe, protect and care for sensitive areas as part of your dog's daily routine.

Common signs can include itching, licking, chewing paws, redness, dry or flaky skin, hot spots, ear irritation, or a dull coat. Some dogs may also rub their face, scratch more than usual, or seem uncomfortable after walks, food changes, or seasonal changes.

Itchy or sensitive skin can be linked to seasonal allergies, food sensitivities, fleas, dry skin, grooming products, environmental triggers, or repeated licking and irritation. If symptoms are severe, ongoing, or your dog seems unwell, it is best to speak to your vet.

You can support your dog by keeping their skin clean and dry, using gentle dog-safe products, supporting their diet with suitable nutrients, checking paws and skin after walks, and avoiding harsh shampoos or irritants. Daily care can help maintain comfort, especially for dogs prone to dryness or sensitivity.

Contact a vet if your dog has broken skin, swelling, bleeding, hair loss, a bad smell, discharge, severe redness, constant scratching, repeated ear problems, or symptoms that do not improve. Skin issues can have many causes, so a vet can help identify the right treatment.
