Spot the safety features

What a safe
scaffold has.

Tap a number on the photo, or pick from the list.

  1. Ladder gate

    A self-closing gate where the ladder meets the platform.

  2. Fully boarded platform

    A complete working surface with no gaps to step through.

  3. Guardrails and toe boards

    Rails at the right heights, and a board at the edge to stop tools falling.

  4. Safe access

    A secured ladder, tied at the top, to every working lift.

  5. No overloading

    Only the people and materials the scaffold was built for.

  6. Secure supports and braces

    Diagonal braces stop the scaffold racking sideways.

  7. Regular inspections

    Checked before use, after bad weather, and at least every seven days.

  8. Strong foundations

    Base plates on timber sole boards so nothing sinks or moves.

Different jobs, different scaffolds

Which scaffold
do you need?

Not sure? We'll recommend the right one when we quote.

The traditional scaffold.

Tube and fitting

Steel tubes joined with clamps. It can be shaped to almost any building, which is why it's used on most houses.

Best for
Houses, awkward shapes, chimneys and roofs
Strength
Built to suit the job

System scaffold

Standard lengths and fixed connection points. Quick to put up on large, regular buildings.

Best for
Large, flat elevations and repeat layouts
Strength
Set by the system's design
Speed
Fast on big, simple jobs

A free-standing tower on wheels.

Mobile tower

Light aluminium towers for short, low-level work on firm, level ground. Not for roof work.

Best for
Indoor work, ceilings, low walls
Strength
Light duty
Speed
Moved by hand around a site

A platform round the stack.

Chimney scaffold

Built off the roof, usually with a front scaffold for access, so the stack can be worked on from all sides.

Best for
Repointing, flashing, pots and cowls

Light duty, built to the stack
Speed
Usually up in a day

Myth or fact

Scaffolding myths,
sorted out.

Tap a card to flip it.

Myth

All scaffolding systems are the same.

Tap to see the fact
Fact

Tube-and-fitting scaffolds, system scaffolds and mobile towers have different assembly requirements, load capacities and uses. The right one depends on the work, the site and the access needed.

Myth

Once it's up, it can be left alone.

Tap to see the fact
Fact

A scaffold must be inspected before first use, after anything that could affect it such as bad weather, and at least every seven days while it's in use.

Myth

You can take a few boards off if they're in the way.

Tap to see the fact
Fact

Only a competent scaffolder should alter a scaffold. Removing boards, rails or ties can make it unsafe for everyone on it.

Myth

Scaffolding is only needed for big jobs.

Tap to see the fact
Fact

Even short jobs at height, like clearing gutters or fixing a few tiles, are safer and often quicker from a properly boarded platform.

Scaffold jargon

The words you'll hear
on site.

So you know what the scaffolders are talking about.

Standard

An upright tube carrying the load to the ground.

Ledger

A horizontal tube running along the length of the scaffold.

Transom

A short tube spanning between ledgers that holds the boards.

Lift

One level of the scaffold, usually about two metres apart.

Sole board

A timber plank under each base plate that spreads the load.

Toe board

A board along the platform edge that stops things falling off.

Tie

A fixing that holds the scaffold to the building.

Scaffold tag

The card that shows when it was last inspected and if it's safe to use.

Common questions

Your questions, answered.

How quickly can you put a scaffold up?

We do our best to fit short-notice jobs in. Most domestic scaffolds go up in a day once booked. Call us with your dates and we'll tell you what we can do.

Do I need permission for scaffolding?

Not on your own land. If any part of the scaffold sits on the pavement or road, a licence from the council is needed. We'll tell you if your job needs one.

How long can I keep it?

As long as the work takes. We agree a hire period up front, and you can extend it if the job runs over.

Who checks the scaffold is safe?

We inspect it before handover and tag it. While it's up it must be inspected at least every seven days and after bad weather.

Will it damage my garden or drive?

Every upright sits on a base plate and timber sole board, which spreads the load. We protect paving and lawns and leave the site tidy.

Can I go on the scaffold myself?

Only if it has been handed over for your use and you're working safely. If you're unsure, ask us first. Never alter it yourself.

What about my neighbours?

If the scaffold needs to go on or over a neighbour's land, you'll need their agreement first. We can help you plan around it.
