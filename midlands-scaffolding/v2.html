Safety first

What a safe
scaffold has.

Read the scaffold guide →
1

Strong foundations

Base plates on timber sole boards, so nothing sinks or moves.

2

Secure supports and braces

Diagonal braces stop the scaffold racking sideways.

3

Fully boarded platforms

A complete working surface with no gaps.

4

Guardrails and toe boards

Rails at the right heights, and a board to stop tools falling.

5

Safe access

A secured ladder to every lift, with a gate at the top.

6

Regular inspections

Checked before use, after bad weather, and at least every seven days.

7

No overloading

Only the people and materials the scaffold was built for.

8

Tied to the building

Ties hold the scaffold firmly to the wall.

Myth or fact

Scaffolding myths,
sorted out.

Tap a card to flip it.

Myth

All scaffolding systems are the same.

Tap to see the fact
Fact

Tube-and-fitting scaffolds, system scaffolds and mobile towers have different assembly requirements, load capacities and uses. The right one depends on the work, the site and the access needed.

Myth

Once it's up, it can be left alone.

Tap to see the fact
Fact

A scaffold must be inspected before first use, after anything that could affect it such as bad weather, and at least every seven days while it's in use.

Myth

You can take a few boards off if they're in the way.

Tap to see the fact
Fact

Only a competent scaffolder should alter a scaffold. Removing boards, rails or ties can make it unsafe for everyone on it.

Myth

Scaffolding is only needed for big jobs.

Tap to see the fact
Fact

Even short jobs at height, like clearing gutters or fixing a few tiles, are safer and often quicker from a properly boarded platform.
